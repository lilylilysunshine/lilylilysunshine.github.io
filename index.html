I study how AI gets from understanding the physical world to acting in it: planning a sequence of steps, respecting exact geometry, and carrying the plan out on real hardware.

My current work uses origami as a test bed. Every fold has to be geometrically exact and done in the right order, which shows clearly where today's multimodal models break down.

Selected work
2026 · Paper under review

Foldable origami: CP2Seq

A paper-folding simulator and benchmark for spatial reasoning. Given a crease pattern and a target folded shape, a model has to produce the fold sequence. The simulator runs each fold and rejects illegal moves with clear feedback.

Finding: across 600 generated tasks, no tested model setup solved a task that needs 11 or more folds. Most failures come from search, not from breaking rules.
